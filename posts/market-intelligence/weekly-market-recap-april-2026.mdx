## Weekly Market Summary

Equities posted gains across major indices this week as a combination of softer inflation data, blockbuster AI earnings beats, and dovish Fed commentary created a favorable backdrop for risk assets. The S&P 500 advanced 2.1% while technology significantly outperformed, with the Nasdaq Composite gaining 3.4%. Bond markets rallied on expectations of extended Fed accommodation, with the 10-year Treasury yield declining 22 basis points to 3.98%.

**Market Regime**: Risk-on sentiment, with high-beta growth and AI-related equities significantly outperforming defensive sectors. VIX traded in the 13-15 range, the lowest level in 18 months.

## Macro Highlights

### Inflation Data Moderates Broadly

The Consumer Price Index released on Wednesday showed headline inflation of 2.8% YoY (down from 3.2% in February), marking the third consecutive month of softening. More importantly, core CPI decelerated to 3.1% YoY from 3.4%, with particular strength in declining services inflation (0.3% MoM pace, slowest in 12 months).

The Producer Price Index followed a similar trajectory, with headline and core both coming in below expectations. Importantly, goods deflation accelerated, with energy prices declining 6% MoM while core goods remained flat — a structural shift that suggests pricing power is eroding faster than many anticipated.

> **Implication**: Inflation is no longer the primary driver of Fed policy divergence. Discussion is pivoting toward labor market resilience versus disinflationary momentum, with significant implications for Fed rate cut timing and magnitude.

### Fed Chair Commentary Shifts Dovish

Chair Powell's remarks at Wednesday's press conference regarding "elevated uncertainty around inflation" and "patience with rate cuts" were interpreted by markets as a green light for Fed easing to commence in June (approximately 75 bps of cuts priced in for 2H 2026). Markets had previously expected no cuts until Q4 2026 or Q1 2027.

This represents a significant repricing of the rate path and is the primary driver of the 22 bps rally in the 10-year yield this week. Two-year yields, more sensitive to near-term policy, fell 35 bps.

### Labor Market Stability Remains

Weekly jobless claims came in at 202,000 (expected 208,000), and the March employment report, released mid-week, showed 165,000 non-farm payroll additions (below expectations of 200,000 but still solid). The unemployment rate held at 4.0%, while wage growth moderated to 3.8% YoY from 4.1% — supporting the Fed's narrative of "two-sided" inflation risk.

## Sector Performance & Themes

**Information Technology** (+4.2%): Driven by blockbuster earnings from AI infrastructure providers. Nvidia (+5.8%) and Advanced Micro Devices (+4.2%) rallied on better-than-expected guidance and customer demand commentary. Cloud providers (Microsoft +3.1%, Amazon +2.4%) benefited from demonstration of AI monetization in their guidance.

**Communication Services** (+3.7%): Meta (+6.4%) rebounded strongly following analyst upgrades centered on AI advertising efficacy improvements. Google (+2.8%) and Amazon (+2.4%) benefited from broad cloud enthusiasm.

**Financials** (+1.2%): Mixed performance as duration risk eased (positive for asset managers and wealth managers) but net interest margin compression concerns (negative for banks) offset gains. Regional banks underperformed.

**Utilities** (+0.1%): Data center power demand, while secular tailwind, is creating visibility around utility capex requirements. Sentiment on the group remains cautious.

**Real Estate** (-1.8%): REITs sold off as the decline in discount rates benefited equities at the expense of yield-dependent assets. Office REITs particularly weak.

## Earnings Highlights

**Nvidia (Earnings Monday)**: Revenue of $31.2B (+233% YoY), exceeding guidance by 12%. Data Center revenue up 252% YoY to $27.8B. Guidance for Q2 FY2026 revenue of $32-33B (vs. estimates of $29-30B) drove stock up 5.8% to $145.20. Management commentary emphasized "no signs of demand abatement" and noted customer concentration remains elevated but stable.

**Microsoft (Earnings Tuesday)**: Revenue $64.7B (+15% YoY), with Azure revenue growth of 32% (vs. expectations of 29%). AI-related bookings exceeded targets by 18%. Cloud segment operating margins expanded to 42% from 38% YoY.

**Meta Platforms (Earnings Wednesday)**: Revenue $36.5B (+24% YoY), beating by 8%. Advertising revenue accelerated to 26% growth from 22% in prior quarter, driven by demonstrated ROI on AI-powered ad targeting. Management notes "AI maturation is driving advertiser efficiency improvements."

**Amazon Web Services (Segment earnings)**: Q1 revenue $25.4B (+21% YoY), with operating margins expanding to 32% from 29% YoY. Generative AI services adoption is 2.5x expected pipeline.

## Portfolio Implications

For growth-oriented portfolios, the combination of moderating inflation, Fed easing expectations, and demonstrated AI monetization creates a favorable environment for continued outperformance. Valuations have not compressed meaningfully despite the rally, suggesting room for multiple expansion if earnings growth materializes.

For value and income-focused portfolios, the decline in rates and rotation toward growth creates near-term pressure. However, the Fed easing cycle may eventually prove beneficial as credit conditions ease and corporate profitability stabilizes at higher growth rates.
